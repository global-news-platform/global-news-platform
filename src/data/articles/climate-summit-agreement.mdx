World leaders have signed a landmark accord committing to a 60% reduction in carbon emissions by 2035, marking the most ambitious climate target in history. The agreement, reached after two weeks of tense negotiations in Geneva, represents a significant breakthrough in global climate diplomacy.

The accord binds signatories to legally enforceable emissions targets, with a mechanism for independent verification and financial penalties for non-compliance. Developing nations secured a commitment of $500 billion annually in climate financing from wealthier countries, addressing a long-standing point of contention.

"This is not merely an agreement; it is a survival pact," said the UN Secretary-General during the closing ceremony. "For the first time, we have a framework that matches the scale of the crisis we face."

The deal was nearly derailed by last-minute disputes over carbon trading mechanisms and technology transfer provisions. A coalition of small island nations, facing existential threats from rising sea levels, ultimately pushed negotiators across the finish line.
