In a landmark decision, regulators have approved a first-of-its-kind gene therapy that could transform treatment for children with a rare genetic disorder. The therapy, developed over a decade of research, targets spinal muscular atrophy and has shown remarkable results in clinical trials.

The FDA's approval came with a requirement for long-term follow-up studies to monitor durability and potential late-emerging side effects. The treatment involves a single intravenous infusion that delivers a corrected copy of the defective gene directly into patient cells.

"This is not merely a treatment but a potential cure," said the lead researcher at the trial's primary site. "We are rewriting the genetic instruction manual for these children."

The manufacturer has committed to an outcomes-based pricing model, where payment is tied to the therapy's effectiveness over time. At an expected cost of $2.1 million per patient, access and affordability remain significant concerns for healthcare systems and insurers.
