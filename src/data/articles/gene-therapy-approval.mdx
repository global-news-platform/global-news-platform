The FDA has approved a revolutionary gene therapy for Duchenne muscular dystrophy, marking a major milestone in the treatment of genetic diseases.

## A Historic Approval

The therapy, developed over 15 years of research, uses a modified virus to deliver functional copies of the dystrophin gene directly to muscle cells. In clinical trials, the treatment showed remarkable results, with 78% of patients demonstrating significant improvement in muscle function.

"This is not just a new drug — it's a new paradigm for how we think about treating genetic diseases," said the FDA Commissioner at the announcement. "We are entering an era where we can address the root cause of devastating conditions."

## Patient Impact

For families affected by Duchenne muscular dystrophy, the approval represents hope where previously there was none. The disease typically affects boys, with most patients losing the ability to walk by adolescence.

"We've been waiting for this moment for years," said the parent of a clinical trial participant. "Our son has gone from being unable to climb stairs to riding his bike again. It's nothing short of miraculous."

## Cost and Access

The therapy will carry a price tag of $2.1 million per patient, raising questions about accessibility. The manufacturer has announced plans for outcomes-based pricing agreements with insurers, and patient advocacy groups are pushing for expanded Medicaid coverage.

## What's Next

Researchers are already working on next-generation versions of the therapy that could be even more effective and potentially treat other forms of muscular dystrophy.
