The Federal Reserve has announced an emergency 50 basis point rate cut following a week of unprecedented market turbulence driven by geopolitical tensions. The inter-meeting move — the first emergency cut since 2020 — comes as global financial markets experienced their worst week since the onset of the pandemic.

"The Committee took this action to support the economy and ensure that financial conditions remain accommodative," the Fed said in a statement. "While the fundamentals of the economy remain strong, risks to the outlook have intensified."

Stock futures reversed earlier losses following the announcement, though gains remained tentative. The S&P 500 had already fallen 8% over the preceding five trading sessions, with the technology sector bearing the brunt of the sell-off.

Central banks in the UK, Eurozone, and Japan issued statements pledging to monitor market conditions closely, raising expectations of coordinated global action. Analysts noted that the Fed's remaining policy tools are more limited than in previous crises, with interest rates already at relatively low levels heading into this cycle.
