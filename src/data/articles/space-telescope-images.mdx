The next-generation space telescope has released its first stunning images, showing galaxies and nebulae in extraordinary detail that surpasses even the Hubble. The images, unveiled during a live-streamed event from NASA's Goddard Space Flight Center, reveal structures in the early universe never before observed.

Among the released images is a deep-field exposure capturing thousands of galaxies in a patch of sky the size of a grain of sand held at arm's length. Another shows the Carina Nebula in wavelengths that pierce through cosmic dust clouds, revealing nascent stars in the process of formation.

"It is as if we have been given a new sense," said the telescope's principal investigator. "We are seeing the universe in a light that is not visible to the human eye, and it is more beautiful and more complex than we imagined."

The telescope, which orbits the Sun at a distance of 1.5 million kilometers from Earth, is expected to operate for at least a decade. Its schedule is already oversubscribed by a factor of six, reflecting extraordinary demand from the global astronomical community.
