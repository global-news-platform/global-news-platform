Stock markets surged worldwide after the landmark US-EU trade agreement eliminated nearly all tariffs, potentially adding trillions to global GDP. The deal, signed in Brussels after 18 months of negotiations, removes tariffs on 97% of goods traded between the two economic blocs.

The S&P 500 posted its best single-day gain in two years, rising 3.8%, while European indices saw similar gains. Industrial, automotive, and technology sectors led the rally, with companies that had been most exposed to tariff uncertainty seeing the largest moves.

"This agreement proves that cooperation outperforms confrontation," the US Trade Representative said at the signing ceremony. "We have built a framework that will generate prosperity on both sides of the Atlantic for generations."

The deal includes provisions on digital trade, intellectual property protection, and regulatory cooperation that go beyond traditional tariff elimination. EuropeanCommissionPresident Ursula von der Leyen described it as "the most comprehensive economic agreement in history."

Analysts cautioned that the benefits would take months to materialize fully, with supply chain adjustments and regulatory harmonization requiring significant time and investment.
