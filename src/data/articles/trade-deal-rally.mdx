Global stock markets surged to record highs today following the signing of a comprehensive trade agreement between the United States and the European Union that eliminates nearly all tariffs.

## The Agreement

The Transatlantic Trade Partnership, signed in a ceremony at the White House, will remove tariffs on over 95% of goods traded between the two economic blocs. The deal is expected to add an estimated $2.5 trillion to global GDP over the next decade.

"This is the most significant trade liberalization in a generation," said the U.S. Trade Representative. "It sends a powerful signal that the world's two largest economies are committed to open markets and mutual prosperity."

## Market Response

The Dow Jones Industrial Average jumped 3.2%, while the S&P 500 and Nasdaq each gained more than 2.5%. European markets saw similar gains, with the FTSE 100 and DAX both reaching all-time highs.

Sectors expected to benefit most include manufacturing, agriculture, technology, and pharmaceuticals — all areas where cross-Atlantic trade had been hampered by decades of tariff disputes.

## Industry Reaction

Business groups on both sides of the Atlantic welcomed the deal. The U.S. Chamber of Commerce called it "a victory for workers, consumers, and businesses."

However, some industry sectors expressed concern about increased competition. Steel and aluminum producers, which had benefited from previous tariff protections, warned of potential job losses.

## What's Next

The agreement now moves to ratification votes in the U.S. Congress and the European Parliament, where it is expected to face some opposition but ultimately pass with bipartisan support.
