Peace negotiations have broken down amid mutual accusations, raising fears of a wider regional conflict and sending shockwaves through global markets. The collapse of talks in Istanbul came after 12 hours of negotiations failed to bridge fundamental disagreements over territorial integrity and security guarantees.

Both delegations have withdrawn from the negotiating table, with each side accusing the other of bad faith bargaining. The breakdown marks the most serious diplomatic rupture since the conflict began, with no new talks currently scheduled.

European leaders have called an emergency summit in Brussels to coordinate a response. NATO announced it was increasing its readiness posture in the region, though officials stressed the move was purely defensive.

"The path of diplomacy remains open, but the window is narrowing," a senior State Department official said. "We urge all parties to return to the table before events overtake us."

Markets reacted sharply, with European indices falling 3-4% and safe-haven assets like gold and government bonds rallying. Oil prices surged above $95 per barrel on concerns about energy supply disruptions.
